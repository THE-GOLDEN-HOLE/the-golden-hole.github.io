Sontag described Camp as a love of the artificial and the excessive: it sees the world in quotation marks and finds beauty in “bad taste”. She named gay men its vanguard: Camp was their way to live inside a culture that refused to take them seriously.

And now — for something serious.

Camp lets you laugh at the serious; it does not abolish it. You can joke about the genre — not about the place where it is made.

What Post-Ost is

After the East

The genre the awards share with the whole world; the place is their own. Post-Ost is the space the twentieth century called the Eastern Bloc, and the people who grew up after it ended. Dozens of languages, four alphabets and one shared history: socialism, the collapse of 1989–1991 and everything that came after.

Post-Ost[pɔst ˈɔst]n. · from Latin post, “after”, and German Ost, “east”
PostPost · Lat. “after”

The prefix of an era: postmodern, postcolonial, post-Soviet. It marks not a break but life among consequences — when the old has ended and the new has not yet been named.

OstOst · Ger. “east”

A word from the Cold War vocabulary: Ostblock, Ostpolitik, Ostalgie. It is the East as named from outside — from the western side of the Berlin Wall. We take the name back and say it from within, the way queer culture once took back the word queer.

Post-Ost is not a state, an alliance or a nostalgia. It is a generation and a way of looking: the shared experience of people who grew up in the wreckage of one utopia.

One map, three ways of seeing

The atlas showed where Post-Ost lies. But every map has someone who drew it, and everything depends on where you look from: what counts as the centre and what as the edge, who is “catching up” and who is ahead. For more than thirty years historians, anthropologists and philosophers have been writing about exactly this — and, as with porn, the idea is not ours. Here are three books we rely on, and what each changes for the awards.

We draw onIWolff · TodorovaIIBudenIIIYurchak

  1. I

    View I / IIIWho called us the East?

    The East the West invented

    The East is a gaze first and a territory only second.

    Wolff showed that “Eastern Europe” was an invention of Enlightenment philosophers: the West needed a half-civilised neighbour in order to feel civilised itself. Todorova traced the same mechanism in the Balkans.

    • 1994Larry WolffInventing Eastern Europe
    • 1997Maria TodorovaImagining the Balkans
    For the awards

    That is why the awards’ first criterion is the author’s gaze from within, not how the scene looks from outside.

    Criterion: the author’s gaze
  2. II

    View II / IIIWhere are we headed?

    Transition without arrival

    The “transition” never ended — it became a permanent condition.

    After 1989 the societies of the region were described as pupils: they had to “catch up” with the West and learn democracy and the market. Buden observed that whole nations stayed history’s eternal adolescents.

    • 2009Boris BudenZone of Transition
    For the awards

    We do not judge authors by the norms of the Western industry. A scene that grew up without studios films on a phone under a ring light — and that is not a lag but its language. It should be described, not corrected.

    Principle: immanent criteria
  3. III

    View III / IIIHow do you live inside a system?

    Living “outside”

    To be inside the system and, at the same time, “outside” it.

    The last Soviet generation dutifully repeated the rituals while building a world of meaning of its own next to them — in kitchens, boiler rooms, samizdat.

    • 2006Alexei YurchakEverything Was Forever, Until It Was No More
    For the awards

    Queer life in the region is still arranged this way. The private Telegram channel is heir to the kitchen and to samizdat: a space “outside” where a culture of its own is born.

    A space “outside”

The three books reach one conclusion: Post-Ost is not how others see us but how we look ourselves.

The awards’ manifesto

Being an author here

Making it alone

That is Post-Ost on the map and in books. This is how it looks from inside, to someone making gay porn here. The “outside” of the third book is no metaphor here: it is a room and a phone. They film, edit and publish on their own — and carry all the risk on their own: no studio, community or institution stands behind the work.

  • Judgement and isolation

    Shame that society passes off as normal: it is safer if neither family nor colleagues know what you do. And there are no studios, schools or festivals where you might meet others like you — everyone starts from zero, almost always alone.

  • Risk and fragility

    In several countries of the region the law calls queer life “propaganda” and porn a crime: being seen can cost you your job, your loved ones, your freedom. Platforms ban, banks refuse, channels vanish overnight — years of work hang on a single account.

  • Silence and oblivion

    People watch the work but never talk about it seriously: instead of a critique, a view counter. This culture has no archive, no criticism, no canon, and what was made disappears with a deleted channel, as if it had never been.

What the awards do

So that no one is alone

This is where an award is needed. It will not repeal laws or change a society in one season. But it can do three things right now — one for each hardship.

  • 01Instead of silence

    Support

    Name, critique, award. In place of silence, a jury’s close response and public recognition.

  • 02Instead of isolation

    Unite

    Bring scattered authors from some twenty countries into one community, with a shared language and shared reference points.

  • 03Without exposing the author

    Make visible

    Make visible what is usually hidden — the work, not the author’s face. This culture has authors, taste, craft and a history of its own — no poorer than the one already hanging in museums.

What we stand on

  1. 01

    Diversity

    Diversity

    Any body, age, type, genre and format. Twenty-odd countries, four alphabets — and no single “correct” aesthetic: “bad taste” is taste too.

  2. 02

    Inclusion

    Inclusion

    The door is open to all: no fee, no threshold of followers or tenure.

  3. 03

    Equity

    Equity

    Equal chances, not identical conditions. A “poor image” shot on a phone competes with a cinema camera, a channel of a hundred followers with one of a hundred thousand, Tashkent with Prague. The jury looks at the work, not at the author’s budget, country or fame.

Why an award

Not a fund. An award.

An award is one of culture’s oldest tools: it selects, it names, and in doing so it makes a canon. We apply it to territory the canon has always avoided — porn that Post-Ost authors film, photograph and draw for their own channels.
